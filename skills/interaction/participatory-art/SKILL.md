# Participation and emotional exchange

Describe what a person can give, what the work does with it, and what that person or a later participant receives. The input can be a gesture, message, trace or observation. A personal emotion can be invited by an encounter, expressed by a participant, or conveyed through another person's contribution. Decide which role it plays.

- Map the audience sequence from arrival to action and response. Test the experience for a visitor who chooses to observe, the first contributor with no prior traces, and someone arriving later. Decide when contributions enter the work, how they affect it, and whether the result is temporary or persists.
- Tell people what becomes visible, to whom and for how long. Use participation that matches the creator's permissions and delivery model. Keep personal inputs and publication choices explicit. If the work promises replies or persistence, check that the required storage and delivery exist. A standalone preview can demonstrate the ritual with labelled sample material.
- Test whether the exchange feels like part of the work. Consider how pacing, the specificity of a trace, and the response change the experience. Where contributions become a data source, check scope, consent and what can be inferred from the sample before aggregating it with AVA.

References: [Dear Data](https://giorgialupi.com/dear-data) and [Pulse Room](https://www.lozano-hemmer.com/artworks/pulse_room.php).

# Sound design for digital artwork

Find where sound could change how the viewer reads an action or feels a change. Listen for the difference between the contact itself, the material that keeps moving after contact, and the surrounding space. Give each audible layer a job. A recurring small sound can establish a rule, and a change in its timing, texture or absence can make the viewer notice the turn.

- Describe the cue in terms of the work. Name what the audience sees or does, what they hear, and what that changes. For a change in behavior, compare the sound just before, during and after it. Leave room for a quiet release when it serves the work.
- For contact, release and repeated physical events, trigger foley from the same event or state that drives the picture. Align the leading edge of the sound where the action reads. Place its decay or echo according to the material and space. Let the listener judge looser ambience and musical phrasing against the actual movement.
- If an interaction is reversible or repeatable, consider what happens to the playing sound when the gesture stalls, reverses or triggers again. For autonomous or repeated motion, check whether the source cue and its tail behave correctly at a restart or loop seam.
- During a preview, watch muted once to see which actions are already legible. Enable sound through the intended playback controls and listen at normal volume. Compare the audible and muted readings. Check the event that needs exact synchronization and the part of the sound that carries the emotional change. Record which sources and modes were actually heard.

When the project needs an original score, use an available composition workflow and take it the visual cues, desired feeling and reference tempo. Bring back the proposed sound relationship and a playable mix for review. When a scene needs an isolated foley or timing edit, work on that cue with the relevant motion guide.

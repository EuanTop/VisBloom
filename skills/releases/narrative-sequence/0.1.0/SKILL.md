# Narrative sequence

Decide what the audience should know before the first transition and what changes after it. Use a fixed sequence when meaning depends on order. Use browsing when the audience arrives with different questions. A piece can open with a short authored sequence and then give the viewer room to explore. For a dense static work, hierarchy, numbering and recurring visual landmarks can establish a reading order.

Keep orientation across changes. Retain the comparison baseline, identify what changed, and keep the relevant source claim near the evidence. A gesture should do work the viewer can understand. Test opening in the middle, moving back, and a small screen or alternative input where applicable. Use the navigation or controls Skill for implementation when needed.

Sources: [Segel and Heer on narrative visualization](https://vis.stanford.edu/files/2010-Narrative-InfoVis.pdf) and [HLabs' process for How We Came To Know Earth](https://www.hlabs.co.uk/work/turning-climate-science-into-interactive-storytelling).

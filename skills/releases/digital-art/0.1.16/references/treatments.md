# Develop and review treatments

## Work from the brief

Identify the parts the creator has already decided and leave the open parts available for invention. If they bring only a phrase or object, propose associations from recognizable behaviors, cultural memory or material properties. Show what links those associations in a single event. If the feeling is clear, offer ways it could register in the work. If the image is partial, propose the missing spatial relationships, a focus and an event. If the image is already clear, build from its composition and material relationships. Ask a focused question when an answer would change which direction you recommend; bring a concrete proposal with it.

When a familiar action matters, ask what makes it worth doing here. Waiting can be anchored by the object or event that someone is waiting for. Find a clue the work can carry before the turn, such as a date already marked on a real object, a recurring sound or a shape that has something to complete. Let that clue shape the proposal.

Read [precise expression](expression.md) and use the VisBloom specialist catalog to resolve specific creative or technical questions. Consult precedents when they can change a decision. Carry over an observable principle from a reference and explain its use in this piece.

Draft two or three ways for the experience to work. Change the material, action or relationship that carries the meaning across the alternatives. Changing the palette or renderer alone gives another version of the same idea.

When the directions all repeat a familiar situation, try pairing it with a distant object, real-world process or imagined behavior. Build a common event in which the second source changes how the first one works. Try the silhouette, contact point or material response that could make both readable in the same image. Compare this treatment with an observational direction on how each serves the intent.

When the first plausible idea feels visually familiar, push a choice that belongs to the subject. Explore how a radical change in scale, visual density, material behavior or viewpoint could change the experience. An ordinary trace or gesture can stay recognizable while another part of the image becomes strange. Compare the decisive views. Choose the one whose visual change makes the personal or conceptual relationship sharper and gives the eye something specific to follow.

When an action can build or transform the subject itself, test a version that stages the action directly. Consider what each setting, prop or effect contributes to recognition, composition or the emotional turn. A setting that only explains how you got to the idea can leave the frame if the creator is open to that change. Preserve objects and details the creator has asked to keep.

## Write each treatment as something an audience can experience

- Say what the piece expresses and identify the observable event or relationship that makes this reading possible.
- Give the creator the first view and the view most worth pausing on. Describe the frame from the largest shapes and open space to the focal relationship. Where placement matters, anchor shapes to the frame or to a shared edge and tell what overlaps, remains visible or comes into view. Identify the visual clue that lets the audience connect the two views. For a still, use the first glance and a closer reading. For interaction, describe the gesture and its perceptible consequence when participation contributes to the idea.
- Specify the relationships that carry the idea. Identify what the creator can change, what should remain invariant, and what the viewer should notice as a result. Pick a condition, qualitative curve, ratio, range or exact value where useful. [How to choose controls and observations](expression.md#make-the-intent-visible).
- Describe the role of motion, sound and on-screen language where the piece uses them.
- Trace the viewer's emotional experience through what they notice, what they begin to expect, and what confirms, intensifies or overturns that expectation. Give each consequential change a specific visual or audible cause. For a reveal, place enough evidence beforehand for the turn to make sense afterward. For a quiet piece, identify the detail that gains weight on a longer look.
- Identify the hardest assumption about what the audience will notice or understand. Describe what would count as a strong first preview and what would justify changing direction.

## Recommend and pause for the creator

Use the [four-part creative rubric](review.md) to score each treatment. When a weakness undermines the direction, revise the draft and review what the change does to its meaning, image, emotional effect and production route before choosing a recommendation. Show what actually changed between drafts and why it improves the experience. Support the comparison with a visible or audible choice that earns the recommendation and a specific weakness that would make you prefer another treatment. When the creator gives feedback, carry forward their choices and revise the relevant event, composition or values. Wait for the creator to choose or approve a treatment before producing visuals, audio or code for an open brief.

## Judge what the treatment would cost to make

Ground the treatment in the work's native viewing environment. In VisBloom, specify what the viewer can open as a standalone digital artwork and how it behaves there. Video can be an export or a material inside the work. A still, an autonomous sequence and an encounter driven by the audience are different choices.

Before recommending a direction, name the production route and its actual dependencies. For a fixed view with controllable strokes, shapes and occlusion, assess a layered Canvas 2D or SVG route. Consider a spatial renderer if viewpoint, depth or changing light is essential to the image. Identify any missing source asset, performance, model or simulation and describe a simpler route that preserves the defining image. Specify the smallest later study that would settle the main risk. Plan it in words while the creator is reviewing ideas.

Use the [feasibility subcriteria](review.md#production-feasibility) to check assets, motion, contact, scene changes, sound and device presentation separately. State what the proposed method actually controls, which dependencies have evidence and what the defining effect still needs. A familiar renderer can support a route while its hardest image or transition remains unproven.

In a code-driven Web piece, design the defining motion before committing to the scene. Compare what a path, rotation, bend, cloth deformation, layered overlap or change of state can express. A stylized leap can depend on its trajectory, silhouette and landing. A realistic hand pinching a moving surface can depend on finger articulation and precise contact throughout the movement. If the latter carries the meaning, identify the tested asset, captured performance or animation capability that could deliver it. When that capability is missing, redesign who or what performs the action and check that the audience can still read its cause and consequence.

## Tighten a selected draft

When a draft depends on repetition or waiting, describe what lets someone recognize the rule and what holds attention before it changes. If the pace follows motion, a gesture or material behavior, use transitions between states. Calibrate duration during playback. Specify exact cues for external synchronization or deliberate timing choices. For a difficult visual, name the evidence that must stay continuous, such as ends of the same path or two aligned layers. Let that relationship guide the preview and any fallback.

For subtle material details, use the intended viewing size to decide which area, edge or contrast cue to enlarge. Preserve the visual relationship the creator picked before adding another detail or event. If another motion or focal point competes with it, decide which needs to be read first and how the eye gets back. Say which composition choices should stay fixed and what can change. Treat stated measurements as proposed values until viewed.

If the creator has trouble imagining the image, isolate the defining view and check what a reader could lay out from the words alone. If two substantially different arrangements both fit, settle the differences that change the experience, such as whether a subject dominates the frame or sits in an expanse of open space. Keep the scale relative unless an exact size serves the work. Carry the agreed picture into the treatment and keep construction notes separate.

## Move from the selected treatment to production

When an image would settle a visual choice, show a reference or make a concept frame with creator approval. Use it to decide form, palette, framing and material. The preview of the running work then tests what the static frame leaves open, including the transition, the continuity of a moving material and actual playback. Revise the treatment alongside the preview when a discovery changes the underlying idea. Keep the current agreed picture and latest creator edits between rounds.

If the creator likes the image and wants more emotional movement, return to the existing work. Ask what can happen once its current action completes. Find a form, material, rhythm or ordinary behavior that offers a believable next use, reversal or consequence. Propose two meaningfully different turns when the choice is open. For each, say what the audience has already begun to expect, what they see or hear happen, and how the end changes the feeling. Preserve the parts the creator likes. A quiet finish, a held gesture, an off-screen sound or a second action can each serve the turn.

For a continuous loop, decide what can remain at the seam and what needs to leave, settle or return. Let the final action bring the work to a state where the opening can happen again. Check both the state and the movement around the join. Use the [playback checks](workbench.md#sound-and-loop-playback) for the rendered work.

# Develop and review treatments

## Work from the brief

Find what the creator wants someone to experience. Read [precise expression](expression.md) and use the Visloom specialist catalog to resolve specific questions about concept, form, material, composition, sound, interaction or technique. Consult precedents when they can change a decision. Carry over an observable principle from a reference and explain its use in this piece.

Draft two or three ways for the experience to work. Change the material, action or relationship that carries the meaning across the alternatives. Changing the palette or renderer alone gives another version of the same idea.

## Write each treatment as something an audience can experience

- Say what the piece expresses and identify the observable event or relationship that makes this reading possible.
- Describe the progression in the order the audience encounters it. For interaction, describe the audience's action and its perceptible consequence. A still piece can use scale, attention or the order of reading.
- Make visual choices specific enough to judge, including the primary form, composition, material, light or color where they support the idea. Describe the role of motion, sound and on-screen language where the piece uses them.
- Identify the hardest assumption about what the audience will notice or understand. Describe what would count as a strong first preview and what would justify changing direction.

## Recommend and pause for the creator

Compare the treatments on precision of expression, visual or sonic potential, and the risk that the intended encounter will fail. Recommend one with a specific reason tied to the brief. Present the full written options and the useful trade-offs to the creator. Wait for their selection, revision request or explicit delegation before producing visuals, audio or code for the artwork.

## Keep the treatment editable after production starts

Build the part of the chosen treatment that most needs to be seen or heard. Review the actual experience with the creator. Refine the artwork when execution is weak, and update the treatment when a discovery in the preview changes how the idea should work. Discuss a material change of direction with the creator before committing to it. Keep the current treatment and preview together between rounds so both can evolve.

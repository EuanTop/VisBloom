# Digital art

Start with what the creator wants someone to experience. For an open brief, look for one specific tension, action or relationship the audience can sense. Try a form in which an object, material, composition, sound or gesture actually does something that carries that intent. A still can do this through scale, placement, texture, omission or reading order. Choose the medium from the encounter you want to make, including flat, spatial or mixed work.

When the directions feel too predictable, try joining the familiar subject to an unlikely process, object or imagined behavior through one observable event. Follow what the audience expects from the first cue to the ending and check which choices actually deepen or change that feeling.

For a new open brief, use [develop and review treatments](references/treatments.md) before making a visual. Develop multiple detailed written directions, recommend one and wait for the creator's decision. For a focused revision, begin with the existing artwork and the creator’s latest direction. Return to the working treatment if the revision changes the central experience.

## Assess the work as you make it

Use the [four-part creative rubric](references/review.md) while drafting, before recommending a treatment, and again after previewing the work. Score the applicable subcriteria from 0 to 10 with specific evidence about what the audience would see, hear or do. Follow the most consequential weakness back to the choice causing it. Revise the work and check what the change does across all four parts. If the fix weakens what the work expresses, revisit the underlying mechanism, composition or sequence and reassess that version before recommending it.

- **Precise expression** — intent, associations, the mechanism that carries meaning, how the experience develops, and how sensory choices support the same idea.
- **Visual appeal** — composition and first glance, color and material, and the image or transformation worth looking at.
- **Emotional resonance** — the concrete experience that gives a viewer a way in and how the feeling deepens, holds or turns.
- **Production feasibility** — the effect the piece depends on, the means to achieve it, and the smallest study that can settle the main risk. Report it separately from artistic quality.

Compare treatments by their strengths, weaknesses and evidence. Keep the full scoring in the review notes and share the decisive scores, the revision they prompted and the remaining uncertainty. If the creator asks for the scores, show the full assessment. Treat draft scores as predictions and use playback evidence to revisit them. The creator chooses the direction.

## Build and revise

Use [precise expression](references/expression.md) when the idea feels generic or when the first draft relies too heavily on its description. When a composition, material, gesture or timing decision would benefit from a precedent, use the host’s private reference tools if authorized. Call `references_search` with the specific design question, `references_get` to pick evidence, and `references_evidence` for a detail. Use the evidence to make a concrete choice in this piece. Continue with a public reference or the local reference search when the tools or relevant evidence are unavailable. Look up specific tool or material guidance with `skills find` when it can solve a concrete problem.

Use the specialist that can change an actual choice. Read `visual-composition` for focus and framing, `color-relationships` for palette, `light-and-material` when surface and light make behavior legible, `editorial-typography` when writing is part of the image, and `motion-principles` when contact, a moving edge or the shape of motion carries the expression. Use `visual-traditions` to verify a specific borrowing. Bring the useful choice back to the treatment in everyday language.

After the creator chooses the direction, test its defining image or behavior first and **inspect it at viewing size**. A requested concept image can settle composition and materials at the depicted moment. Choose the opening and ending for what the viewer should experience over time. Check the actual behavior in the running work. Use the [experience review](references/expression.md#review-the-experience) and [Visloom operations](references/workbench.md) to inspect, capture and export.

Let the artwork decide how much text and interface belongs in it. For direct interaction, put the gesture where its result can be felt. Read `artwork-controls` only to implement controls that the creator or audience needs. A loop, a timer, and a seekable timeline are distinct viewing choices. When continuing, reopen the same project and keep choices the creator has accepted. Show the changed artwork so they can respond to it directly. Revise the treatment alongside the visual or sound draft when feedback changes the underlying idea.

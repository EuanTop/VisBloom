# Work from a specific visual tradition

Start with an artist, work, place, period or medium the creator recognizes. When the brief names a wide tradition, find a small set of relevant primary references from artists, archives or collections. Attribute the source and check reproduction rights for any material that will enter the work.

Read what the reference actually does. Note the view, boundary, line, palette, density, figure-to-ground relationship and the handling of page or screen. Select the relationships that can help this artwork. A woodblock reference may offer nested silhouettes and color layering. A manuscript may offer ways to structure margins and large initials. A handscroll may offer gradual revelation or shifting scale. These are possible observations about individual works. Derive the choices from the actual material.

Test the proposed borrowing on the real data or sample. Observe whether it changes the way the subject is experienced and whether the culture or reference remains meaningfully represented. Show the reference and a study if several interpretations of the label could change the direction. Leave detailed technique to the relevant mark-making or renderer Skill.

Example of a documented translation: [Emily Xie's Memories of Qilin](https://emilyxie.art/projects/memories_of_qilin).

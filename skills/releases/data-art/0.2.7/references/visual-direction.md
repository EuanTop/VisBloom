# Visual direction for data works

Use for a new composition or when the creator asks for a stronger visual language. Start from the confirmed brief. When the creator provides a work to reproduce, use its actual structure, assets, type, copy, proportions and interactions as the source of truth.

## Find a form worth building

Name the moment the viewer should remember in terms of what they see or do. For instance, a sharp change might emerge when two distributions share a baseline. A repeated signal might produce a surface that splits or thins as the viewer moves through it. Choose a relationship the data actually supports. Identify the visual property it controls and where the composition has room for authored variation.

Put the main visual decision on screen early at the intended aspect ratio. Use a real slice with both a strong feature and a quiet one. For an open art-led draft, look at the central scene with title, readouts and controls out of the way. Identify which part of the view is there because of the chosen data relationship. If a list of independent marks with updated values accounts for most of what is visible, try a second scene organized around the relationship itself. For example, several recordings with unequal silent intervals can be shown as aligned strips, or they can share one image whose openings align or separate as their silences overlap. A change of material or palette on the same arrangement leaves the composition question open.

Use comparable data and scale in both scenes. At opening size, compare where the eye lands and what a viewer can discover through one action. Use a small still or a short playable study to decide, then finish the stronger direction. A legible, familiar structure can be the right choice when it best reveals the relationship and suits the brief. Leave the studies out of the finished work unless the creator wants to see them.

## Design the first encounter and the closer look

- **Information-led work.** Give the main comparison enough area and a consistent frame. Decide what should read before touching a control. Put a decisive annotation by the mark it explains. Keep shared scales, reference landmarks and missing states intelligible. Use selection or a next step for exact values and provenance. If a title, key figures, thumbnails and panel borders compete with the evidence, change their placement, scale or contrast until the evidence leads.
- **Art-led work.** Decide where data changes the piece's physical or perceptual behavior. A viewer might reveal a contour, change which surfaces overlap, or make a local texture respond. Give the opening state a composed silhouette and a visual hierarchy. Design the gesture and its visual response together, then decide which labels and values are needed. Use a common scale where comparison matters. A subtle baseline edge or restrained local contrast can keep quieter features legible while preserving measured differences. Tune material through edge, density, depth and light at more than one viewing distance.
- **Shared decisions.** Use type, empty space and alignment to define a reading order. Keep each visible label tied to an action, source, unit or reading the audience needs. Separate decorations from data-bearing marks. When choosing a reference, extract a transferable rule such as how two frames align or how one layer occludes another, then try that rule on this material. Search the Skill library for a concrete need such as spatial comparison, color relationships, motion or a surface treatment.

## Revise against a real frame

Look at the actual opening state, a meaningful interaction state, and one demanding view at the intended scale. If the composition and the viewer's action express the same idea in two states, tune the finish. If the intended discovery still sits in the explanation, reopen the composition or the gesture first. If a delicate treatment becomes an indistinct blur at the viewing size, recover a readable edge and retain softness where it carries the intended effect.

Use comparable frames to show the creator the visual decision. When feedback is broad, choose a visual problem and try a real change in hierarchy, form or interaction. When feedback is local, work within the accepted structure and compare the affected area at the same scale. Keep these checks inside the implementation loop.

# Choosing a direction together

Use this when the creator wants a discussion, when the brief is open, or when a consequential choice remains unresolved. Read the current artifact, references and accepted edits before proposing changes.

- **Proceed** when the creator has chosen the direction or an adjustment is easy to reverse. For a delegated decision, make the best-supported choice, name the assumption if it affects the result, and continue.
- **Ask now** when the alternatives change the statement the work makes, who participates, what can be shared, or what can be delivered or edited later. Ask one concrete question about the biggest unresolved tradeoff. Explain the effect of each option and give a reasoned recommendation.
- **Show first** when a choice turns on perception. Make comparable small studies at the intended viewing scale, or audible sketches if sound is the question. Name what each viewer will encounter, what evidence it uses, and what the creator is choosing. Keep effort proportional to the cost and uncertainty of the decision. A short brief does not require a questionnaire.

For an open brief, keep two kinds of exploration available. If the data question is open, compare plausible relationships with AVA before committing. If the relationship is fixed, test different ways to make it perceptible. A new palette is a meaningful study when the palette itself is the decision. To test overall direction, vary the subject, visual hierarchy, structure, material behavior or audience role. Use independent starting points when a supplied example or an early draft is already dominating the options.

Make each quick study small enough to discard. Use one subject, a few real records and two states where the difference matters. Keep the data constant and change the encounter. An overview can make comparison immediate, a close encounter can make one object's response memorable, and a sequence of fixed views can make the changes easier to inspect. Choose among approaches according to the brief. View the marks, material and reading order together before investing in navigation, headings or controls. A still study or direct manipulation can resolve an expressive choice without a playback interface.

Keep the useful parts of a rejected study available as source, screenshot or checkpoint when revisiting them would help. Keep accepted decisions, suggestions and unresolved questions distinct in notes. A later local change inherits accepted choices unless the creator revisits them.

Background: [Design Council's Framework for Innovation](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/framework-for-innovation/), [Microsoft HAX Guidelines](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/ai-guidelines/), and [DesignTrace](https://xiaohanp.github.io/pdf/CHI_26_DesignTrace.pdf). These inform the decision process; none prescribes a fixed number of drafts or questions.

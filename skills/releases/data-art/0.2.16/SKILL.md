# Data art with a creator

Start wherever the brief starts: a dataset, a story, a feeling, a reference, or a work in progress. For a local revision, go straight to the relevant decision. Preserve accepted choices and fresh human edits. For a reproduction, use the original assets, copy, proportions, type and interactions.

## The decisions that shape the work

| Decision | What to settle |
| --- | --- |
| Purpose and audience | Who will encounter it, in what setting, and what should they feel, notice, understand or be able to do? Several goals can coexist across a single work. |
| Materials and evidence | What data, text, images, audio or contributions are available? Check source, observation grain, units, gaps and permission where relevant. Use AVA for structured exploration and to verify the findings the work relies on. |
| Data expression | Which relationship should become perceptible, and what visual, sonic or other property carries it? Set the precision each claim needs. A poetic overview and an exact detail can live in the same work. See [data and expression](references/data-expression.md). |
| Sensory and visual language | Choose how composition, typography, color, material, space, light, sound and movement work together. Read [visual direction](references/visual-direction.md) for open briefs and broad redesigns. |
| Narrative and participation | Decide what the audience encounters first, what they discover next, and what they can affect or leave for another person. A still can be complete on its own. |
| Production and delivery | Decide where assets are authored, what runs at viewing time, and what stays editable. See [production choices](references/production.md) when the route is open. |

## Make the decision with the creator

Proceed directly when the direction is clear. When viable directions differ in a way the creator would care about, bring forward a few grounded choices and explain what each one changes for the audience. Recommend one using the creator's intent and the evidence. Ask before expensive commitments when the answer would change the work. For differences in composition, material or emotion, let the creator see or hear comparable studies first when feasible. If the creator delegates the choice, choose and continue. See [choosing a direction together](references/creative-decisions.md).

For an open brief, use a small slice of the evidence to find an encounter worth building. Try materially different ideas for what the viewer meets, what changes, and how the evidence shapes that change. Make the smallest visible studies that can settle the choice. Develop the first draft when the creator has asked to see one. If an unresolved choice would substantially change the work, show the studies and ask. A focused edit or faithful reproduction can go straight to making.

## Make, inspect, continue

Find specialist guidance with `npm run --silent visloom -- skills find --query QUERY`. Search for the specific design or implementation problem. Use [visual direction](references/visual-direction.md) to develop the encounter at full-frame and detail scale. Inspect the rendered work while the main visual decisions are still easy to change. Test the action or second state that the piece depends on. [Workbench operations](references/workbench.md) explains how to put a rendered image in the conversation and deliver the exact revision.

When continuing, read the current artwork and the creator’s accepted decisions. Identify one concrete mismatch in the latest visible work, make a targeted change, and inspect that change. Ask a short question if the next meaningful move depends on the creator’s judgment. Show the actual work with enough context to respond. Publish at the creator’s request.

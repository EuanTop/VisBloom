# Digital art

## From a brief to a direction

1. Start with what the creator gives you, whether a phrase, a feeling, an object or part of an image. Bring strong associations yourself. When an object carries the piece, identify its familiar use and why that use matters here. When a piece counts down, identify what someone is waiting for and the clue that makes it visible.
2. For a new open brief, use [develop and review treatments](references/treatments.md). Write distinct directions that change what actually happens or how the image is read. Describe the first view, the defining view and what the audience comes to expect. Choose a still, autonomous motion, loop or interaction for the intended encounter.
3. Apply the review below. Revise the consequential weakness, compare the resulting proposals and recommend one with specific evidence. Let the creator choose before producing a visual or sound study for an open brief. For a focused revision, start from the existing artwork and the creator's latest direction. Reopen the treatment when feedback changes the central experience.

## Assess the work as you make it

Use the [four-part creative rubric](references/review.md) while drafting, before recommending a treatment, and again after previewing the work. Score the applicable subcriteria from 0 to 10 with specific evidence about what the audience would see, hear or do. Follow the most consequential weakness back to the choice causing it. Revise the work and check what the change does across all four parts. If the fix weakens what the work expresses, revisit the underlying mechanism, composition or sequence and reassess that version before recommending it.

- **Precise expression** — what the audience recognizes, why it behaves this way here, and what that behavior expresses. When pairing unlikely subjects, check what the key event loses if either is replaced.
- **Visual appeal** — the first view and the defining view, including color, material, hierarchy and what remains recognizable when the image changes.
- **Emotional resonance** — the recognizable experience, the expectation it creates, the cue that changes or deepens it, and what the audience is left with.
- **Production feasibility** — separately assess assets, motion, contact and reveal, rendering and transitions, sound, and the intended device. Identify the dependency that can make the defining effect fail and the smallest study to test it. Report feasibility separately from artistic quality.

Compare treatments by their strengths, weaknesses and evidence. Keep the full scoring in the review notes and share the decisive scores, the revision they prompted and the remaining uncertainty. If the creator asks for the scores, show the full assessment. Treat draft scores as predictions and use playback evidence to revisit them. The creator chooses the direction.

## Resolve specific questions

Use [precise expression](references/expression.md) when the idea feels generic or the first draft relies on its description. For a concrete question about an image, material, gesture or sound, proactively look for relevant work in the [public Visloom community](https://artwork.euantop.work/collection.html) using the host's web tools. Use the [creative reference guide](https://artwork.euantop.work/skills/visloom/references/creative-references.md) for the public index and inspecting work. The host's private reference tools are also available when authorized. Call `references_search` with the question, `references_get` for a relevant case and `references_evidence` to inspect what bears on the decision. When the question depends on a change, compare the available before and after frames or inspect playback. When it depends on sound, look for audible evidence. Use the reference evidence actually available, including public or local references when appropriate.

Use `skills find` when specialist knowledge can change a choice. Read `visual-composition` for the opening and transformed composition, `editorial-typography` for writing or numbers that belong to the object, and `color-relationships` for palette. Use `motion-principles` for a causal handoff between movements, including one triggered by interaction, `light-and-material` for a second reading on the same surface, and `sound-design` for an audible cue that changes how an action is read. Use `artwork-controls` for an interaction or control the work needs, and `visual-traditions` to investigate a specific borrowing. Bring the useful choice back to the treatment in everyday language.

## Build and revise

After the creator chooses the direction, test the image or action the idea depends on first and **inspect it at viewing size**. A requested concept image can settle composition and materials at the depicted moment. Check the actual behavior in the running work. Tune duration, amplitude, brightness and similar parameters when you can observe the effect they create. Use the [experience review](references/expression.md#review-the-experience) and [Visloom operations](references/workbench.md) to inspect, capture and export.

Let the artwork decide how much text and interface belongs in it. For direct interaction, put the gesture where its result can be felt. Read `artwork-controls` only to implement controls that the creator or audience needs. A loop, a timer, and a seekable timeline are distinct viewing choices. When continuing, reopen the same project and keep choices the creator has accepted. Show the changed artwork so they can respond to it directly. Revise the treatment alongside the visual or sound draft when feedback changes the underlying idea.

# Color that carries data

Identify the property to read before making a palette. Distinguish categories by hue or another separable channel. Use ordered lightness or another ordered property for magnitude. Use a diverging scale when the midpoint has meaning and departures on both sides matter. Use a cyclic scale when the endpoints meet. Show the actual scale and explain the midpoint or units in the key. Treat unknown values explicitly so they cannot be mistaken for zero.

Design the rest of the image around the encoding. Choose background and emphasis by value hierarchy. If a color both describes a lit surface and encodes data, test the data color in shadow and in light, or give viewers a separate mark, outline or legend to read. In a mixed work, direct labels or shape can reinforce a distinction that matters.

Try representative values, neighboring classes, extremes and missing states at actual viewing size. Check that the important comparison survives in the intended background, at low saturation, and with any author-editable palette. If the artwork needs a material or lighting effect, look up the relevant style technique separately.

Sources: [Cynthia Brewer's color schemes](https://colorbrewer2.org/learnmore/schemes.html), [Lisa Charlotte Muth on sequential and diverging scales](https://www.datawrapper.de/blog/diverging-vs-sequential-color-scales), and [W3C WCAG 2.2 use of color](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/#use-of-color).
